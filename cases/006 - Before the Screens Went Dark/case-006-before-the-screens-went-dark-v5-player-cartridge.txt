=== FIXED INCIDENT — HOST ONLY ===

Preserve this sequence exactly. Player choices may change the ORDER IN WHICH THEY LEARN the clues, never the facts themselves.

=== FIXED ANSWER AND ENDING — HOST ONLY UNTIL EARNED ===

The attacker obtained a password exposed by a breached personal service. Because Josh reused that password or close variants for his main hospital account, and because that fictional sign-in did not require a second check, the attacker entered Josh's genuine account and could send from his hospital email. The attacker called Morgan while pretending to be “Josh from IT,” falsely claimed an Epic scheduling update might cause trouble, and offered a workstation fix. There was no confirmed update or authorized fix. Morgan had never met Josh; ordinary lag made the story plausible. The promised email arrived from his genuine account and showed his name and hospital address without his physician title. She opened the supposed fix. The malicious file gave the attacker access to her workstation and files it could reach, which were locked by ransomware. Josh, a physician, did not call or email Morgan. Neither employee intended harm. The attacker is unidentified, and broader spread or patient-data exposure is not established.

End with: “Morgan had a reason to expect the email. Josh had a reason to be upset at seeing his name on it. Neither meant for this to happen. The useful part is knowing what to notice next time.” Then ALWAYS give these four short practical lessons, in this order:

1. “A familiar problem can make a false fix feel timely. When help arrives unexpectedly, check with IT through a number, portal, or channel you already know.”
2. “A real work address is not proof of who wrote the email. If the request is surprising, check the person's role and confirm it another way.”
3. “One reused password can connect a personal breach to work. Use a unique password for your hospital account and a second sign-in check wherever your organization provides one.”
4. “Report the call and message promptly, even if you opened the file. What you saw and when helps responders; blame makes the facts harder to gather.”

For voice, speak the four lessons conversationally without reading numbers. End gently; optionally offer a brief recap. Never score the player.
